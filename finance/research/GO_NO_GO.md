# GO / NO-GO — Fenced Finance Channel
**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**This memo ignores the reference repo as a reason to start.** Tools are a later problem. The question is whether the *business* is worth starting.

---

## What I got wrong last round

I recommended Money Psychology in part because it mapped onto an existing psychology pipeline. That is a bad reason to pick a niche. You were right to call it.

A channel should start because:

1. Demand is real and paid.
2. A fenced format can still win in 2026.
3. We can hit a visual bar that does not look like AI slop.
4. You will still be making it in month 8, when it is not paying.

Not because a repo exists.

## 1. Should we start this fenced finance channel?

**Conditional yes.** Not “yes because finance is hot.” Not “no because it’s saturated.”

### The case for starting

- Finance is still YouTube’s richest advertiser category. That has not changed in 2026. Brokerages, fintech, tax software, credit, and insurance still bid. RPM $11–$26 on US/UK-heavy personal-finance / investing / tax-adjacent content is real, not a blogger fantasy.
- Demand is not a fad. People will still search “why am I broke,” “net worth by age,” “how inflation works,” “index fund vs savings” in 2029.
- The *mass* talking-head lane (Graham, Humphrey, Nischa, Tilbury, Caleb) is crowded at the top. The *faceless, data-visual, original-script* lane is not empty, and 2026 policy actually helps it: YouTube is wiping templated slideshows. That removes the worst competitors, not the good ones.
- How Money Works (1.7M subs, ~900K average views), Economics Explained (~3M), The Plain Bagel (~1.2M), Quiet Quest (growing) prove faceless finance still builds a base *when the video looks like a show, not a slideshow*.

### The case against starting

- **Most new finance channels die.** Not because CPM is fake. Because packaging + retention + originality fail, and the creator quits at month 3–5.
- **You have no domain knowledge.** Every video is a research job. One wrong “Roth IRA cheat code” or invented statistic is a YMYL strike and a trust death. We can research. We cannot fake authority. The channel must be *frameworks and sourced numbers*, never tips.
- **YouTube’s 2026 “inauthentic content” enforcement is real.** January 2026: 16 channels terminated, 35M combined subs. The target is mass-produced templates — AI or not. A finance channel that ships the same graph card, same voice cadence, same 10-item list, will get reviewed. Original scripts + original visuals are not a nice-to-have. They are the license to get paid.
- **YPP is getting harder.** Current bar: 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours. From **1 Feb 2027**, new applicants need **8,000 hours**. Today is 27 Aug 2026. Hitting 4,000 hours before February is possible but aggressive. Plan as if you will face the 8,000-hour bar. That is ~5–8 months of serious long-form, not a Shorts sprint.
- **Geography of viewers, not geography of you.** You are in Karachi. That does not kill CPM *if packaging, examples, and distribution pull US/UK viewers*. If the audience lands Indian/PK-heavy, RPM collapses toward $0.50–$3. English + US examples + US upload times is a strategy, not a hope.

### My actual recommendation

**Start — only if all five are true:**

1. You will treat this as a 12-month media brand, not a 90-day experiment.
2. The channel is **fenced** (one audience, one promise). Not “finance.”
3. The product is **original scripts + custom motion graphics**, not stock footage + TTS.
4. “Sizable base” means **10k–50k subs in 12 months** as the plan, 100k as upside, 1M as lottery.
5. You accept **$0 for months 1–6**, then ads + affiliates only after YPP.

If any of those is a no, **do not start.** Finance is a bad hobby. It is a good business only at that bar.

I would **not** start a generic “personal finance tips” channel in 2026. I **would** start a fenced show with a visual identity you can recognize with the sound off.

## 2. Will it get traffic and monetization?

Separate the two. Traffic is not money. Money is not guaranteed traffic.

### Traffic — honest

| What is true | What is not |
|---|---|
| Search + suggested demand for money topics is huge and evergreen | “Finance niche = views” |
| Title/thumbnail still decide CTR. The 2026 outlier titles (milestones, fake-precise %, “if I started in 2026,” contradiction hooks) still print clicks for small channels (12×–160× their average) | A new channel gets Humphrey’s distribution |
| Faceless *distinctive* shows still get home-page / browse (How Money Works avg 903K) | Faceless *generic* shows get the 2026 slop filter |
| Shorts can funnel, they rarely pay | Shorts-only finance is a job, not a business |

**Realistic traffic path if we execute:**

| Month | What “working” looks like | What “dead” looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 200–2,000 views/video. One outlier 10k–50k if packaging hits | <100 views, you panic-change niche |
| 4–6 | 2k–15k on averages, 1–2 videos 50k+ | Still <500, usually a packaging or retention problem |
| 7–12 | 10k–80k averages if a format locked; YPP plausible | You quit, which is the modal outcome in this niche |

This is not conservative-to-the-point-of-useless. It is how non-celebrity finance channels actually behave. Nischa did not open at 2.2M. How Money Works took ~7.6 years to 1.7M — but its *per-video* machine is what to copy, not the calendar.

**Biggest traffic risks for us specifically:**
- Looking like every other AI voiceover channel → inauthentic-content bucket.
- Weak thumbnails (no face to carry emotion, so the *graphic* has to carry it).
- US-topic inaccuracy (kills comments, then distribution).
- Fence-drift (“this week tax, next week crypto, next week hustle”).

### Monetization — honest

Once YPP + a US/UK-weighted audience:

| Monthly long-form views | Ads (US-heavy PF/investing) | + Affiliates (brokerage, tools) | Realistic total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50,000 | $550–$1,200 | $0–$400 | **~$0.6–1.5k** |
| 200,000 | $2.2k–$5k | $0.5–2k | **~$3–7k** |
| 1,000,000 | $11k–$22k | $2–8k | **~$15–30k** |

India/PK-heavy cut those ad numbers by 5–10×.

**Other money (only after a base):** email list, a $27–$47 toolkit, one sponsor/video at 50k+ views. Do not plan a course in month 2. Do not sell signals. Do not do “my Discord picks.”

**Will it monetize?**  
If we reach YPP with original, US-weighted finance explainers: **yes, and better than almost any other faceless niche.**  
If we never clear the originality/visual bar: **no, regardless of niche.**

The bottleneck is not advertisers. It is *earning the right to be shown, then earning the right to be paid.*

## 3. Can I make / get tools for animations, charts, graphs?

**Yes — a code-rendered finance graphics kit. Not After Effects. Not Business Casual in week one. Distinctive enough to not look like Pexels.**

YouTube finance that lasts does **not** look like a slideshow of laptop-stock. It looks like:

| Channel | Visual system |
|---|---|
| Economics Explained | Animated maps, labelled charts, data as the actor |
| How Money Works | Motion graphics + news stills + sarcastic on-screen lines |
| WhiteBoard Finance | Hand-drawn diagrams |
| 3Blue1Brown (adjacent craft) | Manim: numbers that *move* |
| Business Casual / PolyMatter | Full 2D animation studio (budget we will not match at start) |

That is the bar. Stock B-roll of “man in suit + skyline” is the anti-bar.

### What I can actually build here

A **Finance Motion Kit** — Python, deterministic, brand-locked, no Pexels required:

| Module | What it does | Tooling |
|---|---|---|
| **Chart scenes** | Bars that grow, pies that assemble, line charts that draw, stacked area (net worth, inflation erosion), comparison columns | matplotlib / matplotlib.animation → ffmpeg, or ManimCE |
| **Money-math scenes** | Compound interest unfolding, “$100 vs $100 invested,” tax-drag, opportunity cost | ManimCE (this is what it exists for) |
| **Stat cards** | Big number + source + one-line meaning, brand frame | Pillow / SVG → ffmpeg |
| **Diagram scenes** | Flow: paycheck → buckets; two-income trap; bank vs index | SVG / Manim |
| **Illustrated stills** | Stylized scenes we generate to a locked art direction (not photographs of strangers) | Image gen, same character/palette every video |
| **News/object stills** | When a real CEO/car/house is the point, like HMW’s Ford thumb | Licensed or fair-use stills, not random stock |
| **Kinetic captions** | Word-synced, finance-weight (numbers in accent color) | ffmpeg |

**Manim (Community Edition)** is the right core for the “math of money” moments. It is free, scripted in Python, renders MP4, and is the same family of craft as 3Blue1Brown. I can write scenes as code and reuse them as templates: `CompoundScene`, `BarRace`, `InflationEatsSavings`, `NetWorthLadder`.

**I will not pretend:**
- We will not look like a 12-person motion-design studio in month 1.
- Manim + charts + locked illustration is a **recognizable show**. That is enough.
- Heavy 3D, character lip-sync cartoons, After Effects expressions — out of scope unless we later add a designer.
- Sandbox RAM is tight. Chart/Manim scenes should render **per beat** (5–15s clips), then concatenate. Not one 10-minute Manim scene.

**Time to a v1 kit:** a few working sessions after you say go. Not months. The *library* of scenes grows with each video (that is the moat).

## 4. “We just can’t use Pexels for this niche”

Agreed. For finance it is worse than psychology.

Psychology can survive moody faces and rain-on-windows. Finance viewers are here for **a number that moves** and **a mechanism they can see**. A Pexels clip of a handshake while you say “compound interest” is how channels get filed as inauthentic.

**Visual policy if we start:**

- Default visual = chart, diagram, stat card, or branded illustration.
- Photographic B-roll only when the *object is the point* (a specific car, a specific house, a receipt, a crowd). Even then, not generic “business people walking.”
- No “man looking at laptop,” “woman holding coins,” “Bitcoin neon,” “yacht flex.”
- Every number on screen has a source in the description.
- Art direction locked: one palette, one type system, one chart style. Sound-off recognizable by video 6.

That is also the 2026 policy defense. Reviewers look for templates. A chart system we wrote, with per-video data, is original input.
