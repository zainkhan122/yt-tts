## DESCRIPTION (copy-paste)

Why are deep thinkers — INFJ, INTJ, INFP, INTP — always exhausted? You woke up tired today. And you'll wake up tired tomorrow, no matter how long you sleep — because the exhaustion was never physical.

You didn't lift anything heavy. You didn't run anywhere. But your mind did run: it rehearsed conversations that never happened, replayed the ones that did, read every room, simulated three futures before lunch. That's the invisible labor of a deep thinker — and it runs on a budget.

Carl Jung called that budget psychic energy: a finite supply that every thought, feeling, and act of attention pays for out of the same account. His other insight: directed thinking — aimed, logical, effortful — costs energy like physical labor, while loose fantasy thinking costs almost nothing. Most people live near the stream. You don't get to drift. Your dominant introverted function (Ni, Ti, Fi) holds the steering wheel from the moment you wake up — and the account never gets a deposit large enough to cover it.

In this video: what a day inside your head actually costs, why sleep and vacations never touch this drain, the inferior function that could finally rest you — and five rules of energy economics: externalize the loop, cap the rehearsal, feed the inferior function, ration your depth, and build a real shutdown.

Your tiredness is not weakness. It's an invoice.
