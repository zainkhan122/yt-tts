# Sources — Quibi Raised $1.75 Billion. It Lasted Six Months.

Public record only. If it isn’t here, it doesn’t go in the script.

## Spine (use these)

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Founded Los Angeles, August 2018, as **NewTV**, by **Jeffrey Katzenberg**. CEO **Meg Whitman**. Renamed **Quibi** October 2018 (“quick bites”). | Wikipedia, *Quibi*; Deadline / Dawn C. Chmielewski coverage of the Vanity Fair New Establishment Summit name reveal, 10 Oct 2018 |
| Raised **$1.75 billion**. First ~**$1 billion** in 2018 from Hollywood studios and others (Disney, 21st Century Fox, NBCUniversal, Sony, WarnerMedia, Viacom, Alibaba, Goldman, JPMorgan, etc.). Additional ~**$750 million** by mid-June 2020. | Wikipedia *Quibi* citing WSJ; CNBC/Sherman-style 2018 $1B round reporting |
| Launch **6 April 2020**. US and Canada; limited other markets. Mobile (iOS/Android). | Wikipedia; TechCrunch 6 Mar 2020 launch-lineup piece |
| Price: **$4.99/mo with ads**, **$7.99/mo ad-free**. **90-day free trial** if you signed up around launch (TechCrunch: sign up on the site before 6 Apr; later press: through end of April). | TechCrunch, “Quibi will launch with 50 shows on April 6,” 6 Mar 2020; Cord Cutters News 6 Apr 2020 |
| Episodes **10 minutes or less**. Phone-first; Turnstile (portrait/landscape). | Wikipedia *Quibi*; contemporary launch coverage |
| Day-one: TechCrunch reported **~300,000 downloads**, App Store **No. 3**. | TechCrunch via Wikipedia |
| Katzenberg, May 2020, on the record (NYT): performance “not close to what we wanted”; he attributed what went wrong to coronavirus. **Use as a quote, not as our verdict.** | NYT May 2020, cited on Wikipedia |
| By June 2020: press said the service was on track for **~2 million** first-year subscribers vs a **7.4 million** projection. | Wikipedia citing contemporary reporting |
| Chromecast / AirPlay added **June 2020**. Native **Apple TV / Fire TV / Android TV (Google TV)** apps arrived **October 2020**, days around the shutdown news. | Wikipedia; Variety 1 Dec 2020 |
| Shutdown **announced 21 Oct 2020** (WSJ, then Katzenberg & Whitman). Katzenberg to Deadline: keeping it going “was just going to spend a whole lot more money without any value to show for it.” Last day of service **1 Dec 2020** (Deadline; Variety). | WSJ 21 Oct 2020; Deadline 21 Oct / 1 Dec 2020; Variety 1 Dec 2020 |
| About **500,000 subscribers** at the announcement, per contemporaneous shutdown reporting. | Wikipedia citing Deadline-era figure; Deadline 22 Oct 2020 “500,000 or so sign-ups” |
| Quibi **did not own** the shows. Creators kept copyright; Quibi held **licenses** (IndieWire: seven-year licenses). That is why the library could be sold as rights, not as a studio. | Wikipedia *Quibi* “Shutdown” / “Fate of the library”; IndieWire 8 Jan 2021; CNET 8 Jan 2021 |
| **January 2021:** Roku bought rights to Quibi’s library — **75 programs** — for **less than $100 million**, to stream free with ads on The Roku Channel (rebranded Roku Originals). | Hollywood Reporter / IndieWire / CNET 8 Jan 2021; Wikipedia |

## Villain (one)

**They sold paid, Hollywood, phone-only ten-minute shows into a phone that already gave short video away — and they licensed the library, so when nobody stayed, there was almost nothing to sell.**  
COVID and “no TV app” are chapters. Not the spine.
