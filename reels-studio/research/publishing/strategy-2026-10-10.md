# Hypeless — publishing strategy and operating plan

**Decision date:** 10 October 2026 · **Audience:** global English, US-led (owner-selected).

## Recommendation

**Use explicit scheduled timestamps, not immediate publishing and not an unchecked “Add to Queue.”** Buffer's current GraphQL field is `dueAt` with `mode: customScheduled`; YouTube's direct API uses `status.publishAt`. Both receive UTC timestamps derived from a daylight-saving-aware audience calendar.

Use **America/New_York on all three Buffer channels** and for the long-form planning calendar. Do not use a fixed `EST` / UTC−5 offset. For the owner, Pakistan is **9 hours ahead during Eastern daylight time, 10 during Eastern standard time**. The scheduler computes this rather than hard-coding it.

The Buffer API currently reports **Asia/Karachi** for all three channels and 14–15 available slots on most days, with some only about 15 minutes apart. The exact read-only snapshot is `buffer-schedule-before-2026-10-10.json`. It was not overwritten. Explicit UTC scheduling works correctly even if Buffer's UI keeps displaying Karachi time. Change its display timezone to New York for easier manual operation; timezone does not itself geo-target viewers.

## What the research actually supports

| Evidence | Finding | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Buffer, 1.8M YouTube videos | Shorts strongest on Friday at 16:00, 18:00, 19:00; broader evening pattern. Long-form standout Sunday 10:00, with Tuesday morning and Friday noon useful alternatives. | Median engagement, not a randomized experiment or a forecast for this new channel. Source-normalized local-time recommendations. [2](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/) |
| Buffer Instagram study, 9.6M posts (23 Sep 2026) | Thursday 09:00, Wednesday 12:00 and 18:00 stand out; weekday evenings generally strong. | Mixed post formats, not a Reels-only causal result; January 2024–December 2025 data. [Study](https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram/) |
| Sprout Instagram, nearly 2B engagements across 307K profiles on six networks | Midweek afternoon/evening windows; their Reels guidance follows those broader windows. | Different customer base and engagement definition. [3](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/) |
| Buffer Facebook, 14M posts | Morning advantage; Thursday 09:00 standout. | Not Facebook-Reels-only data. [Study](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/) |
| Sprout Facebook | Broad sample leans midday/afternoon, but its software/tech segment leans weekday mornings. | Explains why generic “best times” lists disagree. We test morning AND later windows instead of pretending one is universal. [1](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/) |

Do not copy third-party articles that reverse the source study's Shorts/long-form findings. Timing is secondary to the topic, opening, retention and thumbnail/title combination. After **28 days**, review comparable post-age views, retention, shares/saves and subscriber conversion by platform/category/slot. Do not compare raw lifetime views, confuse reach with views, or treat one viral outlier as proof. Fixed category-slot assignments can confound comparisons; use controlled topic/slot rotation when testing changes. Automatic analytics-based schedule optimization is **not claimed implemented** in this delivery phase.

## Distribution and SEO architecture

```text
Approved research/brief + Option 3 render + QA + fresh review
                    │
      ┌─────────────┴──────────────────┐
      │                                │
  Shorts queue                    Long-video queue
  Immutable Release assets        Immutable Release assets
  Retained Pages media            Download only to runner scratch
  Buffer: YT / IG / FB             Direct YouTube resumable upload
  Explicit UTC dueAt              Initially PRIVATE
      │                           Processing + thumbnail + playlists
  Confirm Buffer SENT             Explicit UTC publishAt
      │                                │
  Direct YouTube enrichment        Confirm actual PUBLIC receipt
  Backend tags + playlists
```

| Metadata | Buffer shorts | Direct YouTube long |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Native YouTube title; keyword-led caption opening on IG/FB where there is no separate supported title field | Native title, factual hook, under 100 characters |
| Description / caption | Platform-specific copy, catch, source/media credits | Full description, real chapters, sources, credits |
| Hashtags | Relevant platform-specific hashtags | Relevant long-form hashtags; no misleading `#Shorts` |
| Backend tags | YouTube enrichment after Buffer reports sent, using Google OAuth | Set on initial upload; validate the 500-character combined budget |
| Playlists | YouTube enrichment: category + Shorts collection | Category + long-form collection |
| Thumbnail | Existing short-form cover/first-frame design; no claim of custom YouTube Shorts thumbnail support through Buffer | Bold custom thumbnail + 250px review before public scheduling |

IG/FB do not have YouTube-style backend tags/playlists in this API. We do not send invented fields or claim they were applied. YouTube says titles/thumbnails/descriptions matter more than tags; tags chiefly help spelling variants, not guaranteed ranking. [2](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402?hl=en)

**Facebook:** explicitly included by owner instruction on 10 Oct. Always append “AI-generated narration.” The API capability probe checks for native AI-disclosure support and uses the flag if it becomes available. Until then the caption is not represented as a native label or a guarantee of Meta-policy compliance; no claim is made about when Buffer might add support.

## Reliability controls installed

- Separate per-channel calendars; DST conversion; collisions with existing queued/manual posts considered.
- Two-day Buffer horizon, **8 pending/channel operational cap** below the configured Free-plan limit of 10; extra inventory remains in our backlog. No bulk “post now.”
- One long upload/run, at most two new upload sessions/day, three weekly publication lanes, conservative daily quota budget.
- Private-first long uploads; public scheduling only after processing, thumbnail, metadata and playlist checks. Missed timestamps are never sent to YouTube as past `publishAt` values.
- Persisted reservations before non-idempotent writes. Duplicate locks survive render changes; the three manual YouTube uploads remain locked.
- Encrypted resumable-session checkpoints, server-reported byte offsets, recovery after lost final responses; no raw session URLs or OAuth credentials in public git.
- Retry-After, jitter/backoff, persisted cooldowns, bounded requests, explicit permanent-error states. Ambiguous create responses are reconciled, not blindly replayed.
- Playlist-create and membership receipts: a lost response cannot trigger another blind insert.
- Shared Actions concurrency group across Buffer/YouTube delivery. Long and short notifications share a conservative budget; two short notifications/24 h leave room for a long video. YouTube limits viewers to three new-video notifications/channel/24 h. [1](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7389684?hl=en)
- Feature/identity checks, readable reports and explicit “scheduled is not published” status.
- Render releases are now immutable: changed renders need a fresh revision tag, protecting queued files and QA pins.

## Thumbnails and production scope

Main headline: **2–5 words, maximum 6; bold Anton; one clear focal visual; high contrast**. Output 1280×720 plus an actual **250px-wide** preview. The renderer checks text size, contrast, image size and digest binding; the agent must also inspect the mobile preview. Shorten the message rather than shrink the font. Titles/thumbnail claims must match the real demo, not be misleading clickbait.

All narration remains **Option 3: Chatterbox cloning the pinned Michael reference, exag 0.7, cfg 0.4, flow**. Credits remain in descriptions, not under on-screen clips.

This phase builds the sharing/scheduling infrastructure and long-form production contract. It does **not** claim that long episodes have already been researched/rendered, that a dedicated long-form render template is finished, or that fresh stories are autonomously generated daily without approved briefs. The existing radar/agent/cloud-render path supplies content; queues wait rather than publish filler or stale claims.
