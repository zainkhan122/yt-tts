## DESCRIPTION
Toys R Us did not die because children stopped wanting toys. It died on a 2005 loan.

In March 2005, Bain Capital, KKR, and Vornado Realty Trust announced a $6.6 billion leveraged buyout of the Wayne, New Jersey chain Charles Lazarus had started in 1948. The public stock closed for the last time on 21 July 2005. Forbes, reading the deal, put more than $5.3 billion of the purchase price on borrowed money. CEO Dave Brandon’s September 2017 bankruptcy declaration said the overleveraged capital structure had constrained store investment. Press quoting that filing put debt service around $400 million a year in cash.

On 18 September 2017 the company filed Chapter 11 in Richmond, Virginia, to restructure about $5 billion of long-term debt. CNBC put the load around $4.9 billion, with $400 million coming due in 2018. Stores stayed open through a missed holiday. On 15 March 2018 it asked the court to liquidate all 735 U.S. stores. About 33,000 people were on that payroll. The last American locations closed 29 June 2018. The toys kept selling. The specialty chain that owed the 2005 buyout did not.
