## Company

- **Katerra Inc.** Off-site / technology-driven construction. HQ **Menlo Park, California**. Founded **2015**.
- Co-founders: **Michael Marks** (former Flextronics CEO; former Tesla interim CEO) and **Fritz Wolff** (executive chairman, The Wolff Co.). CNBC 24 Jan 2018; Forbes 24 Jan 2018.

## The pitch (the mechanism)

Marks to CNBC, 24 Jan 2018:

> “We bring massive amounts of tech to construction. We have our own factories to make the components. We make sub-assemblies there, bring them to a site, move them around on trucks and conveyors and bring the buildings to life. There’s no handoff. We do everything.”

Forbes, same day, Marks: “We’re a fully-integrated turn-key supplier for developers. So a developer comes to us with a piece of land. We design the building, we engineer the building, and we build the building.” Product example in both Forbes and Wikipedia (from Forbes): entire walls with windows built in, made in a factory, assembled on site.

**Spine:** they bought the architect, the mill, and the general contractor so a jobsite would become an assembly line. Factories only get cheap when they stay full. Construction is lumpy, local, and still a site. Vertical integration meant a cost overrun had nowhere to go except onto Katerra.

## The San Diego hotel (customer record)

**The Information, Cory Weinberg, 31 Oct 2019**, teaser we read:

Cameron Lamming, executive at **RAR Hospitality**, hired Katerra to design and build a **hotel in San Diego**. Katerra had recently raised nearly $1 billion from SoftBank’s Vision Fund. Estimated **half the time** and **5% less**. Lamming: “Given how much money they raised, I wanted to make this bet on them.” **This spring** (2019), Katerra **pulled out** after costs were rising and the timeline dragging. Lamming: **“It’s a good pitch. They didn’t deliver on any of it.”** **“It was a nightmare.”**

GCR 4 Dec 2019, citing that Information article: former employees and customers said Katerra had **withdrawn from at least six apartment and hotel projects** in the US over **higher-than-expected costs**; **more than a hundred** laid off. Katerra spokesperson: “It is common for construction deals to pause or stop for a myriad of different reasons. The changes we have made are a sign of a healthy, but evolving and fast-growing firm.”

Fritz Wolff **left the board** ~Nov 2019 (The Real Deal via GCR 4 Dec 2019). Chapter, not spine.

## 2020–21 death

- **May 2020:** Paal Kibsgaard (former Schlumberger CEO) named CEO, succeeding Marks. Wikipedia cites company PR 19 May 2020. GCR 5 Jan 2021: Marks stepped down May 2020; **400 staff laid off, about 7% of payroll**, a month after (so ~June 2020).
- **March 2021:** Greensill Capital insolvency. GCR 7 Jun 2021; company 6 Jun release calls it “former lender.”
- **1 Jun 2021, The Information (Weinberg):** Katerra **told employees it plans to shut down**. Raised **more than $2 billion** to slash the cost of building apartments. **Expected to let go of thousands** of employees; **likely to walk away from dozens** of construction projects. People familiar. Do not turn “thousands / dozens” into a headcount filing.
- **6 Jun 2021, company PR** (wayback `katerra.com/2021/06/06/katerra-commences-u-s-court-supervised-process-to-implement-financial-restructuring/`):

  Houston, June 6, 2021. Voluntarily filed **Chapter 11** in the **United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas**. Certain **U.S. and Cayman Island** entities. **$35 million DIP** from **SB Investment Advisers (UK) Limited**. International operations **not affected**. Selling **Renovations** and **Lord Aeck Sargent** architecture lines, subject to court. “Katerra recently notified its key stakeholders that **many of its U.S. projects will be demobilizing.**”

  Company explanation (verbatim): “The rapid deterioration of the company’s financial position is the result of the macroeconomic effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on the construction industry, inability to procure bonding for construction projects following the unexpected insolvency proceedings of Katerra’s former lender, and unsuccessful attempts to secure additional capital and business.”

  Advisors: Kirkland & Ellis, Houlihan Lokey, Alvarez & Marsal. Docket: `cases.primeclerk.com/Katerra`.

- **7 Jun 2021** GCR files the Chapter 11 story from that release.
- **4 Aug 2021** VBC PR: court approval to buy Tracy. **10 Aug 2021** GCR: Tracy → VBC; Spokane → Mercer/Blue Varsity.
