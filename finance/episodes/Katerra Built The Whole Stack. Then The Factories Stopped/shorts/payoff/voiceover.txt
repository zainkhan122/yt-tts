Katerra sold a developer a hotel in San Diego. Half the time. Five percent less. Then it pulled out. The client told The Information: it's a good pitch. They didn't deliver on any of it. The paper's sources put Katerra walking off at least six apartment and hotel jobs. It owned the architect, the mill, and the site. So when costs rose, there was no one left to hand the overrun to. They built the wall. They ate the loss.
