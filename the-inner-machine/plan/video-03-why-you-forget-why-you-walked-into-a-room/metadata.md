## Description

You walk into a room with a clear intention, and suddenly you cannot remember what you came to do. It feels like the doorway erased the thought—but the real explanation is more interesting.

This video explains how working memory, attention, context changes and retrieval cues interact when an intention becomes temporarily difficult to access.

The popular doorway effect is not a magical reset button, and it does not mean a brain region literally deletes your goal. A context change can simply make a fragile intention harder to retrieve, especially when distractions are already competing for attention.

We also look at why retracing your steps, saying an intention aloud or leaving a note can help bring the goal back. Forgetting is not always one process, so the useful question is: was the intention never encoded, crowded out, left fragile or missing a cue?
