# Sources — Why MoviePass Died With 3 Million Members
Public record only. No invented dialogue.

## Core timeline
- **Founding:** MoviePass founded 2011 by Stacy Spikes and Hamet Watt. Headquarters New York. (ProductMint, “What Happened To MoviePass,” 2022; WeWork Ideas interview with Spikes, 2018/2020.)
- **Early model:** Spikes said NYC art-house unlimited tickets inspired the idea (conceived ~2005, launched 2011). Early plan charged on the order of tens of dollars a month (Spikes: average about $35/month) and reached roughly 23,000 subscribers before the Helios era. (WeWork Ideas.)
- **Helios acquisition:** August 2017, Helios and Matheson Analytics (HMNY) took a majority stake and announced $9.95/month for one film per day. (Wikipedia, “MoviePass.”)
- **Spikes exit:** Spikes told Business Insider he was emailed 9 January 2018 that his services were no longer needed, after disagreeing with Helios leadership (Ted Farnsworth, Mitch Lowe) on the ~$10 price. (Business Insider, Jason Guerrasio, 13 Apr 2019.)
- **Peak members:** More than three million subscribers by June 2018. (Wikipedia; ScreenRant 2024.)
- **Summer 2018 cash crunch:** Helios filed to raise large sums; late July 2018 the firm said it had to borrow $5 million to continue. *Mission: Impossible – Fallout* weekend: merchant processor stopped advancing ticket funds; many members were blacked out. (Wikipedia; Business Insider, Guerrasio, 2019.)
- **Member collapse:** By October 2018, reports of over a million cancellations. By April 2019, paying subscribers about 225,000 — over 90% below peak. (Wikipedia.)
- **Shutdown:** 14 September 2019, company said recapitalization had not succeeded; service closed. Ted Farnsworth resigned 17 September 2019. (Wikipedia.)
- **Bankruptcy:** 28 January 2020, HMNY filed Chapter 7 and ceased operations. (Wikipedia; Paste Magazine, 2020.)
- **Aftermath (mentioned, not the spine):** November 2021, a New York bankruptcy court approved sale back to Spikes. (Wikipedia; ScreenRant.)

## Mechanism (the villain)
- MoviePass paid theaters **full ticket price** while collecting **$9.95 a month**. One extra visit in a month could cost more than a month of revenue. Growth increased losses. (Earnest Analytics / Dan McCarthy, University of Maryland, 2025; TMS-Outsource roundup citing ~$12–$15 tickets vs $9.95 — treat ticket range as typical U.S. prices of the era, not a MoviePass filing.)
- Early “breakage” hope (gym model: pay but rarely use) failed because the $9.95 plan was used. (FourWeekMBA summary of the breakage problem.)

## What this script does NOT claim
- Exact HMNY share prices (reports conflict after splits).
- Criminal convictions or “they planned a pump” as fact. Lawsuits existed; we do not adjudicate them here.
- Private board quotes except Spikes’s published BI account of the 9 Jan 2018 email.
- That the 2022 relaunch “fixed” the model (out of scope).
