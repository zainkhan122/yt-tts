## Company

- Founded **1949**, Richmond, Virginia, **Samuel S. Wurtzel**, as **Wards Company**. Wikipedia Circuit City.
- Superstore format **1978**; NYSE name **Circuit City Stores, Inc. 1984**.
- Slogan of the service era: **“Welcome to Circuit City, Where Service Is State of the Art.”** Wikipedia (cite needed on wiki — treat as the known slogan, or skip if we cannot find a contemporaneous ad). **If uncited on wiki, do not speak it as a sourced line.** Use “exceptional service / factory-trained staff” which wiki states as “became known for.”
- HQ Richmond. Peak employees **60,000 (2000)** wiki infobox.

## The 2003 floor (the mechanism)

**Stereophile, Barry Willis, 9 Feb 2003, “Cutbacks at Circuit City”:**

- Announced early February 2003. Workforce told **February 5**; **625 stores opened late** that day because of the announcement.
- Dismissal of **approximately 3,900 commissioned salespeople**.
- **10 repair centers** shut. 200 repair-center workers. Service centers 37 → 17 over four years → **7** after this.
- Sales force moves to **hourly**. CEO **Alan McCollough**: save about **$130 million** in payroll **this year**.
- Savings help offset an expected **$40 million** earnings drop from severance and one-time charges.
- About **60%** of Circuit City workers were commissioned.
- About **2,100** of those dismissed **will be replaced by new hires on the hourly system**.
- Dismissals average **three employees per store**.
- December net income **$1.74 billion**, down 5%; same-store sales down 6%.

Wikipedia (same event): converted to a single hourly pay structure, eliminating commissioned sales; many offered hourly “product specialists”; **3,900** laid off; save about **$130 million** a year; “Many company insiders later revealed that they thought this was the most influential company decision that would ultimately lead to its demise.” Stereophile is the contemporaneous primary. Insider-verdict line is wiki — use as “later insiders told the record,” or skip.

**Spine:** they replaced commissioned, factory-trained floor people with cheaper hourly hires to book $130 million. The thing customers could not get at a warehouse competitor was the thing they cut.

## Other chapters (not spine)

- **1988:** opportunity to buy Best Buy for **$30 million**; CEO **Richard Sharp** rejected it. Wikipedia.
- **July 2000:** abandoned large appliances in all stores. Second-largest US appliance retailer behind Sears the year before; **nearly $1.6 billion** appliance sales **1999**. Wikipedia.
- **2000 Horizon** format: more self-serve, red tower entrance dropped.

## Death

- **10 Nov 2008:** Chapter 11, **Eastern District of Virginia**. Reuters Jacobs same day; NYT Rosenbloom 10 Nov 2008; WaPo Ylan Q. Mui 11 Nov 2008. PacerMonitor: **3:08-bk-35653**.
- Approved to borrow **$1.1 billion** DIP (wiki, courier cite).
- Filings: assets **$3.4 billion**, debt **$2.32 billion**; **$119 million** to HP, **$116 million** to Samsung (wiki/Daily Tech/Bloomberg — use wiki’s $3.4B / $2.32B; vendor numbers only if we keep them as reported).
- CEO **James A. Marcum** promised stores would stay open, no liquidation (NYT).
- **16 Jan 2009:** intend to liquidate all stores. **Over 30,000** employees lost jobs in the liquidation (company release via wiki).
- **8 Mar 2009:** original company defunct; full liquidation. Infobox.
- Wiki lists recession, spending, Best Buy as factors. Our verdict stays the 2003 floor. Recession is weather.
