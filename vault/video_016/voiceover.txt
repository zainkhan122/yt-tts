So how do you stop the leak.
Not by thinking your way out.
You have already tried that.
Your depth is not the problem.
The budget is.
This is energy economics, so treat it like economics.
Rule one, externalize the simulation.
Your mind keeps reprocessing anything that stays unfinished.
An open loop in the head pays rent every hour it stays open.
So move the loops out of your head and onto paper.
Write the worry down fully, in plain sentences.
A written loop stops running, because it finally has somewhere to live.
Rule two, cap the rehearsal.
When you catch yourself replaying a conversation for the third time, ask one question.
Is anything new being added.
If the answer is ready, the rest of the rehearsal is just spending.
Rule three, feed the inferior function.
The part of you that is starved of sensation needs raw, physical, present-tense input.
Walk without a podcast.
Cook something with your hands.
Sit outside and name five things you can see.

Rule four, ration your depth.
Depth is your gift, but not everything deserves it.
Not every message deserves a reading between the lines.
Not every mood in the room is yours to process.
Not every future deserves a simulation.
You are allowed to notice something and simply not go in.
Rule five, build a real shutdown.
The mind needs an off-ramp, and off-ramps are built, not found.
Pick an hour each night, write down tomorrow's three loudest loops, and close the notebook.
