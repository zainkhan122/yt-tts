## DESCRIPTION
Why MoviePass died with 3 million members is a public-record story, not a legend.

Founded in New York in 2011 by Stacy Spikes and Hamet Watt, MoviePass sold a movie-ticket subscription. In August 2017 Helios and Matheson Analytics took a majority stake and dropped the plan to $9.95 a month for one film a day. By June 2018 more than three million people had joined. MoviePass paid theaters full ticket price. Members paid less than one visit. The more the company grew, the faster it burned.

Cofounder Spikes told Business Insider he was pushed out in January 2018 after fighting that price. In late July 2018 the parent said it had to borrow $5 million just to continue. Around the Mission: Impossible — Fallout weekend, the merchant processor that advanced ticket money stopped advancing. Members opened the app and hit a blackout. By April 2019 paying subscribers were about 225,000 — more than a ninety percent drop from the peak. On 14 September 2019 the app closed. On 28 January 2020 Helios and Matheson Analytics filed Chapter 7.
