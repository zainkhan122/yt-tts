# Script — Déjà Vu

1. You walk into a new room and something feels strangely familiar.
2. You know you have never been here before.
3. But the feeling arrives with surprising confidence.
4. That experience is called deja vu.
5. It is not proof that you lived this moment before.
6. It is a feeling about memory, not a memory itself.
7. To see why, separate two signals your mind usually blends.
8. One signal says this feels familiar.
9. Another asks where the memory came from.
10. When both agree, recognition feels ordinary.
11. You see a friend and the face brings its history with it.
12. Familiarity and recollection arrive together.
13. Deja vu is different because the feeling arrives first.
14. The sense of knowing appears without a usable episode.
15. You recognize the atmosphere but cannot retrieve the scene.
16. That gap is the important part of the experience.
17. Your memory system has made a signal.
18. Your monitoring system is checking the source.
19. And the source check comes back empty.
20. This is why deja vu can feel certain and impossible.
21. A familiar shape may be hiding inside a new place.
22. The room may share a layout with somewhere you once saw.
23. The lighting, angle or rhythm can overlap without matching exactly.
24. Your mind can register that overlap before you notice it consciously.
25. Familiarity then arrives without a detailed recollection.
26. That does not mean the whole scene was stored and forgotten.
27. It may mean a small cue activated a broad feeling.
28. Prediction adds another layer to the illusion.
29. Once a place feels familiar, the next moment can seem expected.
30. But the feeling of expectation is not evidence of foresight.
31. A strong signal can change how you interpret what happens next.
32. You may remember the hit and forget the many times nothing matched.
33. That is a memory monitoring problem, not a supernatural forecast.
34. Research does not reduce every deja vu experience to one mechanism.
35. Healthy deja vu is difficult to reproduce and measure perfectly.
36. People also differ in how often they notice it.
37. Travel, novelty and attention can shape the opportunities for it.
38. Laboratory memory scores do not capture the whole subjective event.
39. The feeling is richer than a simple remember or forget test.
40. The brain is comparing present input with stored patterns.
41. It is also judging whether the feeling deserves trust.
42. Sometimes those operations briefly disagree.
43. You experience the familiarity without the supporting story.
44. That mismatch becomes conscious as deja vu.
45. The useful reframe is simple.
46. Familiar does not mean previously experienced in full.
47. Certain does not mean correct.
48. And a feeling of prediction is not a prediction.
49. Deja vu is your memory system noticing its own uncertainty.
50. The strange part is that the uncertainty can feel so familiar.
51. Your mind is not replaying the past.
52. It is monitoring a signal in the present.
