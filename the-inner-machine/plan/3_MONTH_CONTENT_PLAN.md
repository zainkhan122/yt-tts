# The Inner Machine — three-month content plan v1

**Planning horizon:** 12 weeks  
**Cadence:** one long-form every 10–14 days; two Shorts per long-form  
**Launch assumption:** English, science-first, 6–9 minute landscape explainers  
**Editorial boundary:** no Jungian personality typing, generic motivation, diagnosis, treatment or “one brain trick explains everything” claims.

## How to use this plan

Each long-form has one viewer experience, one primary mechanism, one counterpoint and one visual model. The two Shorts are not random excerpts: Short A is the cold-open idea rebuilt as a complete 30–45 second discovery piece; Short B is the strongest self-contained payoff. Both use native portrait media, captions and a spoken/text CTA.

The cited links are starting sources, not permission to write from abstracts alone. Before scripting, verify the original paper/review, date, population and limitations in `sources.md`.

# Episode briefs

## Week 1 — Where Do Dreams Come From?

**Viewer experience:** waking with a vivid, impossible story and wondering whether it was random, emotional processing or a message.  
**Promise:** show how dream experience can arise from sleep-stage brain activity, memory fragments and meaning-making without pretending researchers have one settled theory.  
**Mechanism:** REM and NREM dreaming, memory reactivation and cortical interpretation; activation-synthesis is presented as one historical model, not the final answer.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** dreams occur outside REM; the function of dreaming remains debated.  
**Title family:** direct question. Alternatives: “Why Your Dreams Feel Real” / “Your Brain’s Nightly Cinema.”  
**Thumbnail:** dark room, a small film strip dissolving into neural sparks; text **MADE IN YOUR HEAD**.  
**Asset requirement:** 6 landscape storybook scenes, 2 causal diagrams (sleep cycle; memory fragments → dream narrative), 3 abstract transition plates, 2 licensed night/sleep clips; 8 portrait variants for the two Shorts.  
**Short A:** “Every night your brain builds a world with no outside camera.”  
**Short B:** “Why dreams disappear: recall and memory encoding are not the same thing.”  
**Sources:** [S1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21516215/) · [S2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12576410/) · [S3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6022568/)

## Week 2 — Why Your Brain Deletes Most Memories

**Viewer experience:** remembering a face but losing the name, or studying something that feels gone the next day.  
**Promise:** explain forgetting as a competition among encoding quality, interference, retrieval cues and memory change—not a literal storage drive deleting files.  
**Mechanism:** attention at encoding, retrieval competition and cue-dependent recall.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** “forgetting” has multiple forms; a memory can be inaccessible without being erased.  
**Title family:** why + mechanism.  
**Thumbnail:** archive drawers with one glowing label missing; **NO DELETE KEY**.  
**Assets:** 7 scenes, 2 diagrams (encoding → consolidation → retrieval; cue competition), 2 real-world desk/face clips, 4 object macro shots; Shorts need clean vertical archive/doorway compositions.  
**Short A:** “Your brain does not record the day; it records what attention selected.”  
**Short B:** “A missing memory may be missing a cue, not missing from you.”  
**Sources:** [S4](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7644608) · [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38830561/) · [S6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3354918/)

## Week 3 — Why You Walk Into a Room and Forget Why

**Viewer experience:** crossing a doorway and losing the intention that was active seconds ago.  
**Promise:** make the “doorway effect” useful without presenting one popular experiment as universal law.  
**Mechanism:** working-memory goal representation, context change and attentional updating.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** effects depend on task, context and replication; the doorway is a memorable example, not the only cause.  
**Title family:** everyday why.  
**Thumbnail:** person at a doorway, thought-card left behind; **GOAL LOST**.  
**Assets:** 5 scenes, a doorway state-transition diagram, 3 intention cards, 2 portrait doorway loops.  
**Short A:** “The doorway did not erase you; your brain updated the scene.”  
**Short B:** “Why saying the goal aloud can keep it active.”  
**Sources:** [S5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38830561/) · [S6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3354918/)

## Week 4 — The Mechanism Behind Overthinking

**Viewer experience:** replaying a conversation while believing more analysis will finally produce certainty.  
**Promise:** distinguish useful problem solving from repetitive self-focused thought and show the loop visually.  
**Mechanism:** attention capture, threat/value appraisal, uncertainty and repetitive negative thinking; do not reduce it to one network.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** ordinary reflection can be adaptive; clinical rumination is not diagnosed by a video.  
**Title family:** mechanism statement.  
**Thumbnail:** circular hallway feeding back into itself; **THOUGHT LOOP**.  
**Assets:** 6 scenes, loop diagram with exit conditions, 2 “same scene/changed interpretation” shots, restrained abstract sound design.  
**Short A:** “The loop feels like progress because every lap contains a new detail.”  
**Short B:** “The useful question is not ‘what if?’ but ‘what information would change my action?’”  
**Sources:** [S7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3501603/) plus a pre-publication search of current reviews on repetitive negative thinking.

## Week 5 — Why You Procrastinate (It’s Not Just Laziness)

**Viewer experience:** knowing the task matters but choosing a smaller, easier action to feel better now.  
**Promise:** explain procrastination as a time-and-emotion problem, not a moral identity.  
**Mechanism:** present bias, task aversion, mood repair, attentional control and goal conflict.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** neural imaging associations do not prove a single causal brain circuit; context, sleep, workload and mental health matter.  
**Title family:** myth-bust.  
**Thumbnail:** urgent task behind a glowing “relief” button; **MOOD REPAIR**.  
**Assets:** 7 scenes, decision-loop diagram, time-discounting visual, 2 practical but non-prescriptive end cards.  
**Short A:** “The task can be important and still feel emotionally expensive.”  
**Short B:** “The first reward of procrastination is relief—not laziness.”  
**Sources:** [S8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5018960/) · [S9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12514333/)

## Week 6 — What Happens When You Zone Out?

**Viewer experience:** arriving somewhere with no memory of the last few minutes, or watching attention drift during a task.  
**Promise:** show mind-wandering as a change in attention, not a brain “turning off.”  
**Mechanism:** internally oriented thought, default-network participation and switching between task sets.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** mind-wandering can support planning and creativity; it is not always a deficit.  
**Title family:** direct question.  
**Thumbnail:** focus beam opens into a constellation; **NOT SWITCHED OFF**.  
**Assets:** 6 scenes, task-mode/internal-mode state diagram, 3 calm real-world clips, one split-screen visual for helpful versus disruptive wandering.  
**Short A:** “Zoning out is not blankness; it is attention changing its target.”  
**Short B:** “Why a wandering mind can be useful—until the task needs the steering wheel.”  
**Sources:** [S10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3112331/) · [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4427863/) · [S7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3501603/)

## Week 7 — Déjà Vu: When Familiarity Arrives Before Memory

**Viewer experience:** a new place suddenly feels remembered, producing a strong but unreliable sense of certainty.  
**Promise:** separate familiarity from recollection and explain why the feeling can be compelling without being evidence of a previous life or prediction.  
**Mechanism:** familiarity signal, retrieval mismatch and prediction error.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** déjà vu is heterogeneous and its neural causes remain an active research area.  
**Title family:** claim statement.  
**Thumbnail:** split scene—new room, old translucent echo; **FAMILIAR ≠ REMEMBERED**.  
**Assets:** 6 scenes, dual-channel familiarity/recollection diagram, 2 location loops, 4 portrait “new place” shots.  
**Short A:** “That feeling of recognition can arrive without a retrievable memory.”  
**Short B:** “Familiarity is a signal; it is not proof.”  
**Sources:** [S12](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9364811/) plus a current review selected during scripting.

## Week 8 — Why Music Gives You Chills

**Viewer experience:** a chord change produces goosebumps, a chest sensation or an emotional rush.  
**Promise:** connect musical expectation, surprise, reward and body-state interpretation.  
**Mechanism:** predictive processing, arousal/reward and interoception.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** chills are not universal; taste, context and personal memory change the response.  
**Title family:** why.  
**Thumbnail:** waveform becomes glowing skin; **THE NOTE BEFORE**.  
**Assets:** 5 scenes, expectation waveform diagram, abstract pulse animation, licensed music with documented rights, vertical beat-drop variants.  
**Short A:** “The chills may arrive at the moment your prediction breaks beautifully.”  
**Short B:** “Your body helps decide what the music means.”  
**Sources:** [S13](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10029140/) · [S14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12950563/)

## Week 9 — Why Time Speeds Up as You Get Older

**Viewer experience:** childhood summers felt enormous while recent years seem to collapse in retrospect.  
**Promise:** explain why subjective time has more than one mechanism.  
**Mechanism:** attention during an event, novelty, event boundaries and retrospective memory density.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** there is no single “ageing clock” explanation; prospective and retrospective time judgments differ.  
**Title family:** why.  
**Thumbnail:** calendar pages compressing into a single bright mark; **WHERE DID IT GO?**.  
**Assets:** 6 scenes, two timelines (in-the-moment vs retrospective), novelty/repetition contrast, 3 portrait calendar loops.  
**Short A:** “Time can feel fast while it is happening and long when remembered.”  
**Short B:** “Novelty gives memory more landmarks to keep.”  
**Sources:** [S15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9364811/) · [S16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6014633/)

## Week 10 — Why You Can’t Tickle Yourself

**Viewer experience:** another person’s touch can surprise you, while your own movement rarely does.  
**Promise:** turn a familiar joke into a clean explanation of prediction and sensory cancellation.  
**Mechanism:** motor prediction, efference copy and reduced sensory surprise.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** self-generated sensations are not erased; they are usually attenuated and context-sensitive.  
**Title family:** why.  
**Thumbnail:** hand reaches toward a foot while a prediction line arrives first; **TOO PREDICTABLE**.  
**Assets:** 5 scenes, simple motor-prediction diagram, macro hand/texture footage, playful but non-child-directed vertical short assets.  
**Short A:** “Your brain predicts the sensation before your hand arrives.”  
**Short B:** “Surprise is part of what makes a tickle feel like a tickle.”  
**Sources:** use a current peer-reviewed review on sensory attenuation and self-generated touch selected during scripting; do not publish until source is attached.

## Week 11 — Why Habits Feel Automatic

**Viewer experience:** opening an app or taking the same route before consciously deciding.  
**Promise:** show how context-action-reward repetition can make behavior efficient and inflexible.  
**Mechanism:** cue-triggered action selection, reinforcement learning and reduced deliberation.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** habits are not purely unconscious and are not broken by one motivational trick.  
**Title family:** mechanism statement.  
**Thumbnail:** hallway doors with one opening by itself; **THE CUE STARTS IT**.  
**Assets:** 7 scenes, cue→routine→outcome diagram, phone/doorway/commute footage, before/after context contrast.  
**Short A:** “The habit often begins before the conscious decision.”  
**Short B:** “Change the cue and you change the first link in the chain.”  
**Sources:** select a current habit-learning review and primary reinforcement-learning study during scripting; attach DOI/URL in `sources.md`.

## Week 12 — Why Your Mind Wanders When You Need to Focus

**Viewer experience:** a simple task suddenly fills with memories, plans and imagined conversations.  
**Promise:** replace the “lazy brain” story with a competition-and-switching model.  
**Mechanism:** task-control demands, internally oriented thought and context switching; the default network is not simply an on/off distraction network.  
**Counterpoint/limit:** mind-wandering can be useful, and imaging correlations do not determine personal ability.  
**Title family:** contrarian why.  
**Thumbnail:** spotlight leaves a desk and illuminates a distant constellation; **FOCUS HAS AN EXIT**.  
**Assets:** 6 scenes, two-network simplified diagram with uncertainty note, task-switch sequence, calm portrait variants.  
**Short A:** “Focus is not a blank mind; it is a negotiated priority.”  
**Short B:** “The same system linked with wandering can help when the context changes.”  
**Sources:** [S10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3112331/) · [S11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4427863/) · [S7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3501603/)

# Asset and production plan

## Long-form asset budget

For a 6–9 minute video, target **12–18 original base illustrations**, not 20–30 by default. Each base can produce 2–3 genuinely different shots when the crop and motion change the visual information. Add 3–6 short licensed b-roll clips only where real motion improves comprehension. Use diagrams for mechanism claims; do not fill every beat with mood art.

Per episode:

- 12–18 landscape base illustrations, 1920×1080 source or larger;
- 2–4 original explanatory diagrams;
- 3–6 licensed landscape stock clips, with source/license records;
- 3 transition plates/textures;
- 3 thumbnail candidates with distinct composition;
- a complete `assets.json` with provider, prompt/query, license URL, date, checksum, orientation and intended use.

## Shorts asset budget

Each Short receives its own pool:

- 4–6 native portrait AI illustrations, 1080×1920 or larger;
- 2–4 portrait stock clips where available;
- unique queries for hook and payoff;
- no hard crop or blurred landscape fill;
- no sharing of Short hero assets across the two Shorts of the same episode.

## Asset creation decision tree

1. **Can a diagram explain the mechanism?** Create a diagram first.
2. **Does the viewer need a lived human context?** Use licensed stock or an original illustration.
3. **Is the moment emotional/abstract?** Use a generated metaphor, but label it as illustrative in the source/production record.
4. **Does the shot exist only to prevent a static frame?** Remove it or redesign it around comprehension.

## Editorial and technical gates

Before an episode enters production, validate:

- source and confidence for every factual claim;
- no duplicate topic/mechanism in the register;
- landscape project config;
- asset provenance and orientation;
- measured TTS timings and caption schedule;
- per-beat motion and no accidental consecutive asset reuse;
- final `ffprobe` report;
- audio loudness/true peak report;
- title/thumbnail variants and metadata pack;
- AI disclosure decision;
- native portrait Shorts and appended CTA.

## Measurement plan

At 24 hours, 7 days and 28 days log impressions, traffic-source CTR, first-30-second retention, average percentage viewed, average view duration, watch time, top moments, dips, spikes, returning viewers, subscribers gained, comments and end-screen clicks. For Shorts, log viewed-versus-swiped-away, completion and clicks to the long-form where available.

Use the first four episodes to learn which pillar, title family and visual model earns **qualified watch time and comments that demonstrate understanding**. Do not declare a winner from a single CTR or view count.
