## DESCRIPTION (copy-paste)

You say sorry more than you say your own name. Someone bumps into you — you apologize. The waiter brings the wrong order — you apologize. You've even apologized to a chair.

Most of your sorries aren't apologies. They're a question you never stop asking: "Do I have permission to be here?" This is the psychology of people who apologize for existing — and Carl Jung explained exactly why it happens. Your dominant function is feeling: you read the room, soften, and shrink to keep everyone comfortable. But the gift of feeling only works if it has an anchor — and yours doesn't.

In this video: what over-apologizing actually is, the missing function that keeps it running, what it costs you over time, and how to put the word down without losing the gentleness that makes you you.
