# Sources — Toys R Us Didn't Die Because Kids Grew Up.

Public record only. If it is not here, it does not go in the film.

## Company
- Founded April 1948, Washington, D.C., Charles Lazarus, as Children's Supermart / baby furniture. First Toys "R" Us (toys, not furniture): June 1957, Rockville, Maryland. Wikipedia, *Toys "R" Us*.
- HQ at death: Wayne, New Jersey. CNBC, Hirsch, 18–19 Sep 2017.
- Public until the 2005 buyout (NYSE: TOY). Last public close 21 Jul 2005 at $26.75. Wikipedia.

## The buyout (the origin that is the trick)
- 17 Mar 2005: Bain Capital Partners, Kohlberg Kravis Roberts (KKR), and Vornado Realty Trust announce a $6.6 billion leveraged buyout. Wikipedia; Deseret News / AP, 24 Jun 2005 (shareholders approve; $26.75/share; expected close end of July).
- 21 Jul 2005: public stock closes for the last time at $26.75. Wikipedia.
- 28 May 2010: IPO announced. 29 Mar 2013: IPO cancelled, citing market conditions. Wikipedia. The debt stayed.
- Forbes (Vardi, 19 Sep 2017): more than $5.3 billion of the purchase price paid using debt; the three firms and co-investors sank about $1.28 billion of equity. AFR / Bloomberg: ~$1.3 billion equity.
- Vornado's bet was in part real estate (CNBC). Chapter, not spine.

## The $400 million year (the machine)
- Dave Brandon, CEO, bankruptcy-court declaration (quoted by Forbes, 19 Sep 2017): “The company’s overleveraged capital structure has constrained it from making necessary operational and capital expenditures, including investing in the revitalization of stores.” “As a result, the company has fallen behind some of its primary competitors on various fronts.”
- Same declaration / contemporaneous press: about **$400 million a year** in cash to service the debt. Forbes; Wikipedia (bankruptcy section); Retail Dive, 19 Sep 2017 (Brandon: debt service gobbling ~$400 million annually, making it impossible to keep up on store condition, expedited shipping, subscription delivery).
- Forbes: under the owners, capex about $250 million a year; recorded advisory fees to the three owners **$183 million** over the life of the deal (Forbes: majority appears paid); $47 million in transaction fees later waived per company SEC filings.
- CNBC, 18 Sep 2017: retailer has **$4.9 billion** in debt; **$400 million** of interest payments due in 2018; **$1.7 billion** due in 2019.
- Wikipedia: no annual profit since 2013; net loss $164 million in the quarter ending 29 Apr 2017; >40% of sales in the last quarter of the year.
- Forbes: ~40% of product vendors refusing to ship without cash on delivery / in advance; $400 million of debt coming due in 2018; speculation had “started a dangerous game of dominos”; holiday = 40% of revenues.

## Weather (not the villain)
- Amazon, Walmart, Target were taking toy share before and after 2005. Brandon named the capital structure, not a tablet.
- 2000: Toys "R" Us signed Amazon to power its online store (ten-year exclusive). Suit; court ended the deal in 2006. Chapter, not spine. Do not use disputed fee/damages dollars unless we open the opinion. Do not say “Amazon killed Toys R Us.”

## Chapter 11
- **18 Sep 2017**, Toys "R" Us, Inc. files Chapter 11, federal court in **Richmond, Virginia**. Wikipedia; NYT DealBook, 19 Sep 2017; Reuters, Hals/Rucinski, 19 Sep 2017; CNBC, Hirsch.
- Long-term debt **more than $5 billion** to restructure. ~1,600 Toys "R" Us and Babies "R" Us stores worldwide still operating; workforce **64,000** (Reuters / Wikipedia). Canada parallel CCAA. Operations outside U.S. and Canada **not** in the case.
- DIP financing commitment **over $3 billion** / **$3.1 billion**, JPMorgan-led syndicate. CNBC; Forbes.
- Brandon press line, CNBC: “Today marks the dawn of a new era at Toys"R"Us where we expect that the financial constraints that have held us back will be addressed in a lasting and effective way.”
- Forbes: Brandon estimated ~$96.5 million in fees just to procure the DIP (Lazard conversations). Mention only if we have room; not the spine.

## The holiday miss → liquidation
- Retail Dive, Emma, 18 Sep 2018 (*One year later*): holiday sales fell **more than 10%**; missed Q4 sales and profit targets on the bankruptcy loan → covenant default; lenders extended into March, then chose liquidation. Filed without a pre-negotiated store-closure / debt-removal plan.
- Jan 2018: liquidate/close up to **182** U.S. stores (Wikipedia; Retail Dive ~180).
- **15 Mar 2018**: company seeks court approval to wind down U.S. business and liquidate inventory in **all 735 U.S. stores**, including Puerto Rico. ~**33,000** U.S. employees. Orlando Sentinel, 15 Mar 2018 (company release); Tiger Group recap of the motion. Brandon: “we no longer have the financial support to continue the Company’s U.S. operations.”
- Liquidation sales begin **23 Mar 2018**. Online store to closing notice **29 Mar 2018**. Wikipedia.
- **24 Apr 2018**: Canadian division sold to Fairfax Financial for ~**$234 million**; 82 stores keep the name. Wikipedia.
- Isaac Larian / MGA tried to buy a large U.S. remnant. It did not hold. Chapter, not spine.
- **29 Jun 2018**: remaining U.S. locations permanently closed. Wikipedia. NYT 29 Sep 2023 (WHP flagships): “Toys ‘R’ Us closed the last of its 735 U.S. stores by June 2018.”

## What is *not* the spine
- Geoffrey the Giraffe (trademark — do not generate, do not fake).
- 2019 Tru Kids IP transfer; 2021 WHP Global; Macy’s shop-in-shops; American Dream / Mall of America flagships. The **name** outlived the **735 U.S. warehouses**. This film ends at June 2018.
- 2018 Congressional letters (Warren, Sanders et al.); $20 million hardship fund; 2020 TRU Creditor Litigation Trust suit (Brandon bonus, board fees). Real. Not this mechanism.
- Do not flatten $4.9B long-term vs ~$5B vs Warren’s “$7.9B debt vs $6.6B assets” into one number. Use CNBC $4.9B / “about $5 billion long-term” / $400M a year as Brandon’s cash.
- Do not assert a specific “kids grew up / iPad killed retail” study as a filing. It is the folk explanation the title answers. The filing answers with debt service.
