# Sources — Jawbone Lost The Wrist

Public record only. If it is not here, it does not go in the film.

## Company
- **AliphCom, Inc.**, San Francisco. Founded **1999** by **Alexander Asseily** and **Hosain Rahman** (Stanford). Wikipedia; Fortune, Lashinsky, 22 Jan 2015.
- Noise suppression with Lawrence Livermore; **Navy and DARPA** grants, 2002. Wikipedia; Fortune 2015.
- Wired headset 2004 (flop after Jobs savaged the clip — Fortune, Rahman’s recollection). Bluetooth **Jawbone** headset **2006**. Company used the Jawbone name from **2008**; AliphCom renamed Jawbone **2010**. Wikipedia; Fortune.
- **Jambox** wireless speaker and **UP** fitness tracker **2011**. Wikipedia; Fortune.
- Yves Béhar, chief designer. Wikipedia infobox; Fortune.
- Liquidated **July 2017**. Wikipedia; TechCrunch, Heater, 6 Jul 2017; Bloomberg, Wang, 10 Jul 2017; FT, Bradshaw, 7 Jul 2017.
- Rahman moved to **Jawbone Health Hub** (clinical, new investor). TechCrunch 6 Jul 2017 citing The Information. **Chapter, not spine.**

## Money
- Fortune 2015: raised **more than $400 million**; last year’s round at a valuation of **more than $3 billion**; ~**450 employees**; “no closer to making money” than a decade earlier; “hundreds of millions in revenue.” Investors named: a16z (Horowitz on the board), Khosla, Sequoia, Kleiner Perkins, Silver Lake, BlackRock.
- Headset years: Fortune says **sizable profits in 2007 and 2008** after Cingular/AT&T stocking late 2006; 2009 sales dried up with the financial crisis.
- Jan 2016: **$165 million** more (Recode via The Verge, Goode, 27 May 2016). Verge: total raise to date **$1 billion**.
- TechCrunch 6 Jul 2017: **around $951 million** over the years (PitchBook); BlackRock funds had pumped **$300 million** about a year before liquidation. Names: a16z, Sequoia, Kleiner, JP Morgan, Mayfield, Khosla.
- Attribute PitchBook “around $951 million” as TechCrunch’s figure. Do not invent a 10-K.

## The band (the machine)
- First **UP (2011)**: production snafu, malfunctions; **full-price refunds**. Fortune 2015; The Verge 27 May 2016.
- **UP3**: heart-rate; **sat out Christmas 2014** on a production/waterproofing glitch. Fortune 22 Jan 2015. Verge: lukewarm reviews when it shipped.
- Fortune 2015, on the record before Apple Watch: “Execution misses and slow-footed strategic moves have left Jawbone vulnerable to disciplined competitors, including the surging Fitbit… scramble for cash and struggles to ship a quality product on time.”
- Fitbit cheaper similar bands; Apple Watch as weather. Not “Apple killed Jawbone” as the whole autopsy. The filing of the death is they **stopped making the consumer band**.

## The stop, then liquidation
- **27 May 2016:** Verge (Goode), independently confirming: trying to **sell the audio business**; sources that a **clinical-grade** heart wearable was a last chance; Tech Insider via Verge: **stopped production of UP**, remaining inventory sold to a third party. Jawbone declined to comment except to refute that it was exiting wearables.
- Last Jambox **2013**; last headset **early 2014**. Verge.
- June 2016 Rahman blog “we’re still committed” (TechCrunch 2017 citing jawbone.com). Social accounts silent from early 2017; customer complaints unanswered.
- Customer-service agency cut after nonpayment (Business Insider via Forbes 2019). Chapter.
- **6 Jul 2017:** liquidating. Sherwood Partners handling. Fitbit litigation continues as leftover. TechCrunch. Health Hub is a **new** company, new investor, similar name.
- BBC 2 Jul 2018: remaining trackers pulled from shops. Aftermath.

## Legal (chapter, not spine)
- Fitbit trade-secret suits; 2016 judge for Fitbit; 2018 federal charges against some Fitbit employees (Verge). Do not try the case. Do not make it the villain.

## What is *not* the spine
- Jobs meeting as the death.
- Health Hub / 2021 Samsung patent NPE.
- “Wearables died.” Fitbit and Apple were still on wrists.
- Do not invent a Chapter 11 if the record is assignment/liquidation via Sherwood.
