## DESCRIPTION
Two incomes made many families poorer in freedom even when the spreadsheet got bigger. This video shows the two-income trap as stacked bars: housing, childcare, transport, and status costs that were bid up because two paychecks showed up to the auction. The second income did not land in savings. It landed in a higher fixed-cost floor. That floor is brittle — two jobs, two commute, two careers that cannot both break without the house wobbling. We draw a then-versus-now cost stack using historical household data and the argument popularized in Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi's work on the two-income trap, labeled as research not scripture. This is not an argument that a partner should quit. It is an argument that a second paycheck spent entirely on newly mandatory costs is not wealth. Wealth is what remains when one bar disappears. Education, not financial advice. Look at the chart, then look at your own fixed costs. Which of them exist only because the second income exists?
