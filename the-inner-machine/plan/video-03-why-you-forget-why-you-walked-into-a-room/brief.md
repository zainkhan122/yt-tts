# Video 03 brief — Why You Walk Into a Room and Forget Why

## Audience experience
You enter a room with a clear intention, then the doorway seems to erase it. The viewer wants an explanation that feels useful without turning one popular “doorway effect” story into a universal law.

## Promise
Explain how goal representations, attention, working memory and context changes can make an intention temporarily harder to retrieve.

## Primary mechanism
A context change can update the active mental scene. When attention is divided or the goal is weakly maintained, a new context competes with the prior intention.

## Counterpoint / limit
The doorway effect is not a guaranteed everyday law; task difficulty, expectation, environmental cues and replication matter. The explanation is broader than one brain region or one experiment.

## Visual thesis
A person carries an intention card through a sequence of rooms. At each context change, the card can remain bright, become obscured or regain visibility through a cue.

## Voice direction
Warm, conversational and precise. Target 140–145 WPM; pause after “the doorway did not erase you” and before the final practical reframe.

## Short candidates
- Hook: “The doorway did not erase you; your brain updated the scene.”
- Payoff: “A small cue can bring the intention back because the goal was not necessarily deleted.”
