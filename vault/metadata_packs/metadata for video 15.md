## DESCRIPTION (copy-paste)

Why do INFJs push people away when they need them most? The Fe–Ti loop under stress, explained through Jung.

There's a pattern you've never named: every time you're hurting the most, you disappear. You cancel plans, stop replying, say "I'm fine" — and then wonder why you feel so alone.

Here's the part that stings: they didn't abandon you. You locked the door, then got angry that no one broke in.

This is the psychology of pushing people away when you need them most — and Carl Jung's map explains exactly why it happens. Your leading function is extraverted feeling: you feel everyone else's needs so intensely that your own need feels like a burden, so you pre-emptively withdraw. Then introverted thinking rationalizes it as strength: "handle it alone." That's the Fe–Ti loop — and it's running your lowest moments.

In this video: what the pattern looks like, the two functions that drive it, what it costs you over the years, and the exact first move to break it — one honest sentence.
