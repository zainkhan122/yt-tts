## DESCRIPTION (copy-paste)

Everyone confides in you. The coworker. The cousin. The friend of a friend you met once. Within ten minutes they are crying, and you know their childhood — and when you are falling apart, you scroll every contact and find no one.

This is the psychology of the friend everyone confides in: the Helper persona. Extraverted feeling built a one-way valve. You receive other people's processing. You were never taught to hand any back. You are fluent in being needed and illiterate in being known.

Carl Jung named the mask that makes you useful: the persona. For you, the persona is the Listener — calm, wise, always fine. The tool is not a lie. The trouble starts when the tool becomes the face. Then their feelings are the news, and yours are background noise. People confide in the Listener. They rarely befriend the person underneath.

In this video: a typical week of one-way friendships, why everyone picks you (intuition, feeling, or thinking — the pattern holds), Jung's rule that you can only take another person as far as you have traveled yourself, a thirty-second inventory of five "friendships" that may be five appointments, the three rents the role charges (exhaustion, lost language, underground resentment), and four ways to keep the gift without living in the cage: start with your own news, return what you cannot carry, train two people to ask twice, and listen on purpose.

You were a person before you were a function. This week, one honest sentence to one person. Watch what happens when the Listener finally speaks.
